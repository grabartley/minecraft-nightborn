# ADR 0011: Vampire structures share one structure type

## Context

The PRD has five kinds of vampire structure: castles, mountain castles, crypts, ruined manors and underground lairs (section 5.13). Each must be rare, fit the land, hold several vampires and loot, and sit in fog that thickens towards its heart (HOME-1 to HOME-4, HOME-8). A data pack must be able to change layouts, loot, rates and biomes (HOME-12, COMPAT-5). Placement must not load extra chunks (PERF-6), fog must cost nothing away from a structure (PERF-4), and fog must never hide a threat within 8 blocks (ACC-4). The operator sets structure rates (CFG-5).

More Doors places its vaults with a custom jigsaw structure type and a custom random spread placement that reads its config each time it is asked (More Doors ADR 0065). Nightborn follows the same shape.

## Decision

**One structure type, `nightborn:vampire_structure`, serves every kind.** It takes the same fields as a vanilla jigsaw structure plus a `household`: the entity type that lives there and how many. Its `size` must be at least one, because vanilla's jigsaw generator places no pieces at all at a size of zero. Biomes, terrain adaptation, layouts (template pools), processors and loot all stay in data, so a new kind is data only. No code names a kind.

**The first piece placed is the heart.** Residents settle on open floor inside it, and fog is thickest at its centre. A layout puts its throne room, blood pool or crypt chamber in its start pool. A household can name a `head`, an entity type with its own stage odds. The head settles first, on the sheltered floor spot closest to the heart's centre column, with the lowest such spot winning a tie, so a layout puts its throne there. A head with `"seat": "highest"` takes the highest such spot instead, so a layout can keep its head in the top room of a tower. Residents keep clear of the head's spot. Every resident and head settles only where an opaque block above keeps the sun off, so none starts the day burning in a courtyard. A household can set a `ceiling`, which counts a roof only that many blocks over the head of every resident and of the household's head, so a layout with deep eaves or overhanging battlements keeps its residents indoors.

**Residents settle once, from the chunk that holds the heart's centre.** `postPlace` runs once per chunk, so only that chunk spawns the household, and only on floor inside both the heart and that chunk. Residents are persistent so they stay home. `HouseholdSpots` keeps them apart while there is room.

**Rarity is read as chunks generate.** The `nightborn:rare_spread` placement type has the fields of vanilla's random spread plus a `rarity` that names the setting it reads: Vampire structure sets name `vampire_structures`, which reads `structureRarity`, and hunter camps name `hunter_camps`, which reads `campRarity`. Its spacing and separation are the data's numbers scaled by that setting, from 25% to 400%. As in vanilla, the data's spacing must be larger than its separation. Changing the setting only affects chunks not yet generated.

**A structure can ask for good ground.** An optional `footing` samples the terrain across the whole footprint every `spacing` blocks, 8 by default, before the structure is placed. It refuses the spot if any sample is under water, when `dry` is set, which it is by default, or if the ground rises more than `max_rise` blocks across it. A refused spot gets no structure, the same as a biome that does not match. A footing can also name an `entrance`: a jigsaw block the template carries in the floor of its gate or door, facing out. Vanilla takes a jigsaw structure's height from the ground under the middle of its start piece, which leaves a door at the edge hanging above a slope. When an entrance is named, the whole structure is moved up or down after it is laid out so that floor sits level with the ground one block outside it. A structure with a `summit` is also moved sideways so that the ground before its entrance is the peak itself: the mountain castle's gate opens onto the top of its mountain, and its rock base fills down the far side. A footing may cap the move with `max_lift`, and a spot that would need more gets no structure. A structure that names an entrance its pieces do not carry gets no structure either. The ground outside the entrance is checked with the rest of the footing, so a gate never opens onto water. The castle, mountain castle, ruined manor and crypt each name theirs, and the mountain castle moves 10 blocks at most so its keep stays on the peak. The underground lair has no way in from the surface, so it uses a `cave` instead of a footing.

**A structure can ask for a summit.** An optional `summit` samples the ground every `spacing` blocks out to `reach` blocks from the chunk it was offered. It starts the structure on the highest sample between `min_height` and `max_height` instead of on the chunk corner, and refuses the spot only when no sample is in that range, so a tall layout never reaches the build limit. A layout names a jigsaw at the point that should stand on the peak with `start_jigsaw_name`. The biome is checked at the peak, so a chunk beside a mountain can still seat a structure on it. The structure start stays in the chunk that was offered.

**A structure can open onto a cave.** An optional `cave` names the jigsaw in the floor of the structure's passage, like a footing's `entrance`, and the structure is moved until that passage opens onto a natural cave. It tries every move out to `reach` blocks sideways, every `spacing` blocks, and `rise` blocks up or down, nearest first. A move is taken when the floor just outside the passage is solid with two open blocks above it, the cave carries on past it without a drop of more than one block, there is no water or lava within 3 blocks, and at least `approach` cave floors within 12 blocks walk to the passage, stepping up one block or dropping at most three. The floor is never lower than `lowest`. A spot with no such cave gets no structure. The cave is read from the noise the terrain is built from, before any chunk exists. Vanilla's own column sampler builds the whole height of the world for each column, which made a failed search take seconds, so the search runs vanilla's chunk noise sampler itself, one chunk at a time, and only fills the blocks between the lowest and highest floor it can try. It keeps the full world height for the sampler's shape, because the aquifer estimates the surface by scanning down from the top of that shape. Carvers and cave features run after this choice and can still take away a floor or grow a stalagmite in the way, so the search also picks a route of `path` steps from the passage into the cave, and a small `cave_path` piece makes that route walkable when each chunk is built: it lays the `sill` block where the floor is gone and clears whatever stands in the two blocks above. A cave cannot be combined with a footing or a summit. The three live together in one part of the codec, `Siting`, which also keeps the structure codec within its sixteen fields.

**Reach is capped by the codec.** `max_distance_from_center` plus the padding terrain adaptation adds plus the summit or cave `reach` must stay within 128 blocks, the 8 chunks a structure start references. A layout that reaches further fails to load rather than loading extra chunks.

**The server finds fog and the client draws it.** Fog covers the structure start's box, which already includes vanilla's 12 block terrain adaptation padding, and a 24 block margin around that. It is full at the heart and fades with distance from it, and it fades to nothing across the margin, so it never starts abruptly. Once a second the server looks at the structure references already held by loaded chunks within two chunks of each player, which always covers the margin. It reads a start only from a loaded chunk, so it never loads one. When the zone a player is deepest in changes, it sends the heart and box. A player with no zone costs one map lookup per nearby chunk each second, and the client does nothing. The client eases towards the target and pulls vanilla's fog in towards 20 blocks at the heart. It never pushes back a nearer vanilla fog, such as blindness or water, so a threat 8 blocks away is at most 40% faded.

**Ambience rides on the fog zone.** A structure's optional `ambience` takes vanilla's biome effect fields: a looping `ambient_sound`, one-shot `additions_sound` with a `tick_chance`, and `music` with `min_delay`, `max_delay` and `replace_current_music` (true unless set). A kind's soundscape is data like everything else. Sounds live in the client's resource packs, so the server cannot check the ids, and a wrong one only shows up as an unknown sound warning in the client log when it would play. The server sends the ambience with the heart and box, so it costs nothing more. The client fades the loop in and out at a fortieth of full volume a tick and holds it at the player's closeness to the heart, plays one-shots only inside the zone and louder towards the heart, and gives the structure's music in place of the game's. Outside any zone, gothic music takes the place of the game's own music at night, letting a playing track finish. Menu, credits, underwater, Ender Dragon and End music are never replaced.

## Consequences

- Castles, crypts, manors and lairs (issues 70 and 71) are data files on this type. A castle is one rigid piece, 41 blocks square, with the keep's throne at its exact middle so the lord, its household's head, settles before it. It stands on a two block plinth with thin beard terrain adaptation, and its footing keeps it to dry ground that rises 6 blocks or less. Household routines (issue 69) build on the residents settled here.
- The mountain castle (issue 173) is one rigid piece, 63 blocks square and 96 tall, on the same type. Its summit search puts the keep's foot on the highest ground within 48 blocks that lies between y 100 and y 250, and its footing refuses a peak that falls away more than 30 blocks under it. It uses no terrain adaptation: 32 blocks of rock under the upper ward and 26 under the lower fill the gap to the mountainside, with a skirt of rock that falls away outside the walls. Its yards are bare stone, so no tree or grass grows in them. Its roofs step every other course so that vanilla's snow can lie on them in cold biomes.
- A household holds up to 32 residents. All of them settle in the chunk that holds the heart's centre and live within the home radius of it, 16 blocks by default, so a large layout keeps its coffins inside that reach. The mountain castle sleeps its twenty in three floors of the keep around the heart's centre. The Eldest's room is the top floor, more than a home radius above the centre, so no resident claims its coffin.
- A head that keeps to a room, as the Eldest does, shares the household's home but is leashed to the spot it settled on, looks for its coffin only there, and never goes out to feed.
- A ruined manor is one rigid piece, 48 by 44 blocks, standing in dark forests. Its template sets every block of its box, so the trees and hills it lands on are cleared to its own garden, and thin beard terrain adaptation fills the ground beneath its plinth. Its footing keeps it to dry ground that rises 5 blocks or less, and its exclusion zone keeps it 6 chunks from any castle, which favours the same woods. Its rooms are joined by doors and stairs all the way from the gate to the turret study, and a unit test walks them the way a player would to prove it.
- A crypt is a church on the surface hung from the crypt below by a vertical jigsaw. The crypt is the start piece, so it is the heart. Any buried rigid piece makes vanilla shape the ground around it, which cut into the churchyard, so crypts use no terrain adaptation. Instead the crypt piece is a level bank of earth under the whole churchyard, faced with a rubble retaining wall, and its footing keeps it to dry ground that rises 4 blocks or less. Its placement has a `near` rule, `exclusion_zone` turned around: a crypt only starts within 8 chunks of a chunk where the village grid can start a village, and its exclusion zone keeps it more than 6 chunks away, so the church stands just beyond the edge of a village. The rule asks the village placement, not the biome, so a crypt can rarely stand by a grid spot where no village grew; crypts keep to the same plains villages favour, which keeps that rare.
- A lair is one deep piece, a den around a blood pool or a grotto with a spring and a coffin ledge, cut into the rock beside a natural cave, about y -50 to -5, so no sun reaches it. The piece is its own cave: a rounded floor plan under a domed, dripping roof, with solid rock two blocks thick at its walls and roof filling the rest of its box, so it uses no terrain adaptation and aquifer water stays outside it. One narrow passage runs through that shell to the edge of the piece, and its `cave` siting moves the lair until the passage opens onto a natural cave a player can walk in from. It is placed in the last generation step, after vanilla has set its water springs, so no spring opens in its walls. Its household goes out through the cave to hunt at night like any other (HOME-13). Its exclusion zone keeps it 8 chunks from any trial chamber start, so it never cuts into a chamber. Mineshafts are scattered by chance rather than on a grid, so no rule can keep a lair clear of them, and one can rarely cut a mineshaft tunnel short. It has no village rule, and its spacing of 40 chunks makes up for the spots that have no cave near, so lairs turn up about as often as they would with 48 chunk spacing and no cave rule.
- A heart whose start chunk is not loaded casts no fog until it loads. With a view distance of two or more this does not happen inside a structure.
- Fog changes distance only, not colour, so it reads as mist by day and darkness by night.
- Vanilla's structure locator caches misses per chunk until restart, so `/locate` can stay wrong for searched chunks after the rarity changes, as More Doors found.
- Changing the rarity in a world that is already explored moves the grid. A new start in fresh land can reach into chunks that already generated and be cut off there, and two structures can sit closer than their separation where new land meets old.
- Every layout guarantees the same things on foot, and a unit test proves each for every layout. A person and a vampire mob walk the same routes: stairs, never ladders, with two blocks of headroom, the height of the tallest shipped vampire build. Every coffin, chair, table and lectern a household uses is reached by the mob walk. No floor under a roof is sealed off. Every door leads to walked floor on both sides. Wall decor hangs on solid wall and floor decor stands on solid floor. Hollow roofs and turret cones are filled, so no attic is left empty.
- Blood stains are placed by the generator, not by hand. `tools/structures/src/main/resources/blood_stains.json` sets how many clusters each kind has, how wide they are, how many stains and pools each holds and how high on a wall a stain may sit. Each template is stained from a random seeded by its path, so it comes out the same every time. Stains keep off doors, stairs, ladders and the cells beside coffins.
- The test server turns structure generation off, so natural placement is checked by hand. Gametests cover settling, the codec cap, the rarity scaling, the fog lookup, the ambience the zone carries and the loot table.
